The whole relationship, start to finish.

A subcontractor comes on board, gets approved, stays compliant, works jobs and gets paid. SubSub handles every one of those stages, so none of it lives in someone's inbox.

Send an invite. They do the rest.

You add a company and an email. The subcontractor gets their own login and builds their own profile — so you aren't retyping details off a business card or chasing them by phone.

  • Trades and capabilities — roofing, siding, windows and doors, gutters, soffit and fascia, coping, decks and fences, hardscaping.
  • Where they'll work — named cities, or travel radii from ZIP codes.
  • Their crews and who's on them, so you schedule a real crew rather than a company name.
  • Licence, bond, insurance and signed agreement, uploaded from their phone camera.

Approve them properly, not by eyeballing a PDF.

Filing a certificate isn't the same as reading one. SubSub puts your requirement beside the document, records who approved it and when, and keeps the subcontractor off jobs until each item clears.

  • Coverage checked line by line — general liability, aggregate, products and completed operations, auto, employer's liability, umbrella.
  • Licence verified against the state rather than self-reported — live against the WA L&I registry, which also reports the bond and insurance on file.
  • Confirm you're named as additional insured, primary and non-contributory.
  • Accept a limit that falls short when it suits the scope — with a reason, recorded against your name.

Compliance that keeps itself current.

Certificates lapse and registrations expire without anyone mentioning it. A subcontractor who was clear in March may not be in September, so SubSub watches the dates instead of you.

  • Expiry tracking on every policy, bond and registration, flagged before you dispatch anyone.
  • Automatic reminders by email and text, pointing them at their own portal to upload — never a reply-to-this-email thread.
  • Held back until cleared. Anyone with an outstanding item can't be assigned, so it isn't a judgement call on a busy morning.
  • A record that holds up — who approved what, on what date, at which limits.

Put the right crew on the right job.

Enter the job once — address, square footage, schedule, materials, measurements — and pick the trades it needs. SubSub ranks who can actually do it, and the work order writes itself when you assign.

  • Matched by trade, distance, availability and rating, with anyone non-compliant out of the running.
  • Crew-level availability — each crew keeps its own calendar, so you see which crew is free that day, not just whether the company is.
  • One work order per trade, numbered and dated. A three-trade job issues three.
  • They accept or decline from their phone — or with auto-schedule on, the job books straight onto their calendar.

Pay them for the workComing soon

The last piece still living outside the system. By the time a job closes, the amount owed is already known — the work order set it when the crew accepted.

  • Pay against the work order, at the value agreed when the crew accepted it.
  • Release on completion, once the job is marked complete and the crew rated.
  • Payment history per subcontractor, alongside their jobs, ratings and documents.
  • Year-end totals already assembled, because every job was priced in the system.

Two sides of the same platform.

Managing subcontractors usually fails because it's one-sided — you end up holding everyone else's paperwork. In SubSub they have their own accounts and maintain their own records, which is the only version of this that stays current.
