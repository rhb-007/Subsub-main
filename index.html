The whole relationship, start to finish.

On board, approved, compliant, working, standing behind the work, paid. SubSub handles every stage, so none of it lives in someone's inbox.

Send an invite. They do the rest.

You add a company and an email. They build their own profile from there.

  • Nearly 30 trades — framing and concrete through electrical, drywall, tile and paint — plus the cities or ZIP radii they cover.
  • Their crews and members, so you schedule a crew, not a company name.
  • License, bond, insurance, W-9 and agreement, uploaded from their phone.

Approve them properly, not by eyeballing a PDF.

Filing a certificate isn't reading one. Your requirement sits beside the document, and they stay off jobs until it clears.

  • Coverage checked line by line — general liability, aggregate, products and completed operations, auto, employer's liability, umbrella — plus that you're named as additional insured, primary and non-contributory.
  • License verified against the state — the WA L&I registry also reports their bond and insurance.
  • Accept a short limit when the scope suits it, with a reason on the record.

Compliance that keeps itself current.

Certificates lapse quietly. Someone clear in March may not be in September.

  • Expiry tracking on every policy, bond and registration.
  • Automatic reminders by email and text, pointing them at their portal.
  • Held back until cleared, with a dated record of who approved what, at which limits.

Put the right crew on the right job.

Enter the job once — address, size, schedule, materials, measurements — and pick its trades. The work order writes itself when you assign.

  • Matched by trade, distance, availability and rating. Non-compliant subs don't appear.
  • Crew-level availability, so you see which crew is actually free.
  • One work order per trade, each with its own scope and value.
  • They accept or decline from their phone against a deadline you set.
  • Change orders, not edits. Found rot on tear-off? A numbered CO the sub accepts, and the revised value is derived — the original work order never changes.

Every labor warranty, tracked by job.

Each sub states how long they stand behind their work. SubSub holds it against every job, so two years on you know whose problem a failure is.

  • Warranty term on their profile — 1–10 years or lifetime, set by them.
  • Callbacks vs warranty claims — inside 30 days it's a callback, later it's their term.
  • Coverage shown on the job — "within 10-year warranty, 8 years remaining", or expired.
  • They confirm the return visit, so it lands on their schedule.

Pay them for the workComing soon

The last piece outside the system. The work order already set the amount when the crew accepted.

  • Pay against the work order, at the value agreed, with history per sub.
  • Release on completion, once the job is closed and rated.
  • Year-end totals already assembled.
