# SubSub vs Buildertrend

**Short answer:** Buildertrend runs the whole residential project, homeowner included. SubSub runs the subcontractor relationship: onboarding, insurance verification, compliance, dispatch, work orders and labor warranties. If you want one system for everything, pick Buildertrend. If your problem is the subs, pick SubSub. Plenty of GCs use both.

## At a glance

| | SubSub | Buildertrend |
|---|---|---|
| Built for | Managing a subcontractor network | Running residential construction projects end to end |
| Sub self-onboarding (profile, crews, documents) | Yes, from their phone | Sub/vendor portal; process is yours to configure |
| Insurance checked line by line against your requirements | Yes | Not the core of the product |
| Expiry reminders to subs (email + SMS) | Yes, automatic | Configure your own process |
| Non-compliant subs blocked from assignment | Yes | No hard block by default |
| Matching by trade, distance, crew availability, rating | Yes | No |
| Labor warranty tracked per job | Yes | No |
| Estimating, client portal, Gantt, financials | No | Yes |
| Published pricing | Yes | No, quote-based |
| Price | Free (3 subs) / $99/mo unlimited | Third-party estimates ~$339 to $1,000+/mo |
| Sub logins | Free, don't use a seat | Included |

## Where Buildertrend wins

- **One system for the whole job.** Estimates, selections, schedules, client communication, budgets and invoicing in one place.
- **Homeowner experience.** The client portal is a genuine strength for custom builders and remodelers.
- **Maturity.** Large user base, lots of integrations, lots of people already trained on it.

## Where SubSub wins

- **Compliance that enforces itself.** Coverage is checked against your requirement, line by line, including additional insured and primary and non-contributory. Lapsed subs can't be assigned.
- **Subs keep their own records.** They upload license, bond, insurance, W-9 and agreement themselves, and get reminded before anything expires. Washington registrations are checked against the L&amp;I registry, which also reports the bond and insurance the state holds.
- **Dispatch by who's actually available.** Crew-level availability, matched by trade, distance and rating. The work order writes itself on assignment.
- **Labor warranties by job.** Two years on, you know whose problem a failure is and how much term is left.
- **Price.** $99/month for unlimited subs, users and jobs, published on the site.

## Can you use both?

Yes, and it's a common setup: Buildertrend for the project and the client, SubSub for the sub network. On Scale, SubSub's CRM connector (Zapier, Make, n8n or API) can bring scheduled jobs in so nobody retypes them.

## Which should you choose?

- **Choose Buildertrend** if you're a residential builder or remodeler who wants one platform for estimating, scheduling, client communication and financials.
- **Choose SubSub** if you already run jobs somewhere and your pain is insurance, compliance, finding available crews and tracking whose work it was.

## FAQ

**Is SubSub a Buildertrend alternative?**
For subcontractor management, yes. For estimating, client portals and financials, no. It's designed to sit alongside a tool like Buildertrend.

**Is SubSub cheaper than Buildertrend?**
Yes. SubSub is free for 3 subs and $99/month for unlimited. Buildertrend is quote-based; third-party estimates start in the hundreds per month.

**Does Buildertrend track subcontractor insurance?**
You can store sub documents in Buildertrend. SubSub's difference is checking coverage against your specific requirements, chasing renewals automatically and blocking non-compliant subs from jobs.
