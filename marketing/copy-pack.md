# SubSub launch copy pack

Scripts and posts for the growth campaign. Everything here describes what SubSub
does today; nothing promises a feature that is still being built. Where a line
needs a link or a name, it is in `[brackets]`.

**Links to use**

| Who | Link |
|---|---|
| A sub inviting a GC | their own referral link, from **Get your GCs on SubSub** in the app (`subsub.work/gc?ref=CODE`) |
| A GC inviting another GC | their own link, from **Account → Refer a GC** (`subsub.work/gc?ref=CODE`) |
| A GC inviting a sub | the invite link from **Subcontractors → Add → Invite** in the app |
| Anyone, cold | `subsub.work/subs` (subs) or `subsub.work/gc` (GCs) |

Using a referral link is what credits the referral: a sub earns $100 and the
Preferred Sub badge when the GC they brought in starts paying, and two GCs each
get a month of Scale free.

## 1. SMS invite scripts

Send these **from your own phone, to people you already work with.** That keeps
them personal and keeps them lawful: a text to somebody you know, about work you
do together, is a different thing from marketing texts to a list. SubSub never
texts anybody on your behalf.

**Sub → GC, after they ask for your COI**

> Hey [name], just sent my insurance through SubSub. The link always shows the current certificate, so you won't need to ask again. You can run your subs on it too: [link]

**Sub → GC, at renewal time**

> [Name], my COI renews next month. If you're on SubSub you'll get the new one automatically, nothing to chase. Free to look: [link]

**Sub → GC, after a job wraps**

> Thanks for the work on [job]. I keep my license, insurance and W-9 on SubSub. It's how I send paperwork to every GC now, takes seconds: [link]

**GC → sub, onboarding a new sub**

> Hi [name], it's [you] at [company]. We schedule and send work orders through SubSub. Set up your free profile here so I can send you the next job: [link]

**GC → sub, chasing paperwork**

> [Name], your insurance on file with us expires [date]. Upload the new one here and it updates for every GC you work with, not just us: [link]

## 2. GC onboarding emails

Three emails, sent on day 0, day 3 and day 7 after a general contractor or
property manager signs up. The sender is a person at SubSub, not a no-reply
address.

### Email 1 · Day 0 · "Your first three subs"

**Subject:** Get your first three subs into SubSub

Hi [first name],

Welcome to SubSub. The quickest way to see what it does is to put three of your
real subs in. It takes about five minutes:

1. Open **Subcontractors** and press **Add**.
2. Type a sub's name and email, or scan the SubSub code on their phone if they already have one.
3. They get a link, upload their own insurance, license and W-9, and you never
   type their paperwork again.

Once they're in, every expiry date is tracked for you. If a certificate is going
to lapse under a job you've booked, SubSub asks them for the renewal before it
happens.

Reply to this email if anything gets in the way; a person reads every reply.

[Your name]
SubSub

### Email 2 · Day 3 · "What's expiring"

**Subject:** Which of your subs' certificates run out this quarter?

Hi [first name],

Most GCs find at least one lapsed certificate in their first week on SubSub.
Not because anyone hid it, but because it was in a PDF from last spring.

Open **Dashboard**: subs whose documents are missing, expired or waiting on
your review are listed there. One press asks the sub for the new one, and when
they upload it, it's current everywhere.

In Washington, SubSub also checks every sub's L&I registration every night,
bond and insurance included. If a registration lapses, you'll know before
they're on your roof.

[Your name]
SubSub

### Email 3 · Day 7 · "Send your first work order"

**Subject:** Send a work order and let the sub pick the time

Hi [first name],

Here is the part that saves the phone calls. Create a job, pick a sub and send
the work order. The sub accepts it and confirms a time, and if a tenant has to
let them in, the tenant is asked last, once the crew is booked. Nobody turns up
to a job nobody agreed to.

One more thing: if you know another contractor who'd use this, your referral
link is under **Account → Refer a GC**. When they start paying, you both get a
month of Scale free.

[Your name]
SubSub

## 4. Contractor Facebook group posts

Written to read like a contractor talking, because they are meant to be posted by
one. **Post them as yourself, and keep the disclosure line.** A post that hides
who wrote it breaks the FTC's endorsement rules and most groups' own rules on
self-promotion, and it's the post that gets you removed from the group. Check
each group's rules before posting, and answer replies as yourself.

**Post 1: the COI question**

> Honest question for the GCs here: how many times a year do you ask the same sub for the same certificate of insurance? I counted last year and it was embarrassing. I've moved my subs onto a shared profile where they upload once and I always see the current one, with the expiry date. Curious what everyone else does. Spreadsheet? Shared drive? Just trusting it?
>
> (Full disclosure: I'm with SubSub, the tool I'm describing. Happy to answer questions either way.)

**Post 2: for subs**

> Subs: do you have a folder of PDFs you email to every GC who asks for your paperwork? I did. Now I send one link that always shows my current insurance, bond and license, and when my COI renews, every GC I've sent it to gets the new one. It's free for subs. If you've got a better system, I'd like to hear it.
>
> (Disclosure: I work on SubSub, which is what I use for this.)

**Post 3: the handyman limit**

> Something I didn't know until I looked it up: the most a handyman can charge without a contractor license varies a lot by state. $500 a job in Washington, $1,000 in California, no exemption at all in a few states, and nothing at the state level in Texas. Plumbing and electrical aren't covered anywhere. I put together a free lookup by state if it's useful: subsub.work/handyman-limits. Not legal advice, check your state board.
>
> (Disclosure: the lookup is on SubSub's site. I work on SubSub.)

**Post 4: checking a sub's license**

> PSA for Washington GCs: you can check any contractor's L&I registration, bond and insurance in about ten seconds before they start. I check every new sub now after one showed up with a registration that had lapsed two months earlier. Free lookup here: subsub.work/check/wa
>
> (I'm with SubSub, who made the lookup. It reads L&I's own public records.)

**Post 5: scheduling with tenants**

> For the property managers here: what's your process when a repair needs a tenant to let the crew in? We kept booking a time with the tenant and then finding out the crew couldn't make it. We flipped it: the crew confirms first, then the tenant is asked. Fewer no-shows, fewer "nobody told me" calls. How does everyone else handle it?
>
> (Disclosure: I work on SubSub, which is how we do this now.)
