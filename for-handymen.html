From sign-up to the first job.

Sign up yourself, or open the link a property manager sent you.

  1. Sign up, or open the link you were sent

    On the sign-up form, pick Subcontractor or handyman. If a property manager invited you, their link already has your details and you only choose a password.

  2. Add your W-9

    Add your liability certificate too if you carry one; property managers like seeing it. Nothing else is needed to be taken on as a handyman.

  3. Get taken on as a handyman

    The property manager or owner sets that on their side. It covers the everyday trades: painting, drywall, flooring, tile, carpentry, cabinets, doors and windows, gutters, decks and fences, landscaping, cleaning, and small plumbing and electrical repairs.

  4. Confirm the time

    Accept the job, confirm the time or propose another, and see who lets you in and where to meet them.

  5. Log your hours

    Send each day's hours. The property manager approves them or sends them back with a reason, and nothing is paid that they haven't seen.

Where the handyman line is.

Every state draws it differently. This is the short version; the limits page has every state.

  • Most states set a dollar limit per job. Labor and materials together, and it ranges from a few hundred dollars to tens of thousands.
  • Splitting a job doesn't get under it. The whole job counts, not each invoice.
  • Some work is outside every exemption. Plumbing, electrical and HVAC beyond small repairs, gas, and structural work need the trade license whatever the amount. Work that needs a permit usually voids the exemption too.
  • Some states license nobody, and some exempt nobody. In the first, your city or county may still have rules. In the second, unlicensed work isn't exempt at any amount.
  • Not legal advice. Check with your state's licensing board before relying on any figure.
