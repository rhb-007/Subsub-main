Rolling it out across sites.

Without a six-month implementation.

  1. Set the standard centrally

    Your coverage schedule, bond minimum and required documents become the baseline for every contractor.

  2. Import the contractors you already use

    A company name and an email each. They receive logins and build out their own profiles and documents.

  3. Review once, use everywhere

    Each document is verified against the standard once, and that approval applies wherever the contractor works.

  4. Give every site the same list

    Unlimited users on Scale, so each property manager works from the same verified set of contractors.

  5. Watch the exceptions, not the folders

    Your attention goes to renewals due and documents awaiting review, rather than to filing.

What changes at scale.

The differences that only show up past a handful of properties.

  • Coverage areas do the sorting. Contractors set the cities or radii they serve, so each site sees who can actually reach it.
  • Ratings accumulate across the portfolio. A crew that performs at one property is ranked higher at the next, per crew rather than per company.
  • License checks run against the state, so a lapsed registration surfaces without anyone at site level noticing it.
  • Exceptions are auditable. Every accepted shortfall carries a reason and an approver, which is what a portfolio review actually asks for.
  • Unlimited users and contractors on Scale at $99 a month, with contractor logins free.

Everything else you get at portfolio scale.

One standard across sites is the reason to start. Everything below applies at every property, from the same contractor records.

Onboarding

Invite a contractor and they build their own profile — trades, coverage area, crews and documents. You aren't retyping anything.

Document approval

Insurance, bond, signed agreement and W-9, each checked against your requirements line by line. Accept a limit below requirement with a recorded reason.

License verification

WA L&I registrations checked against public state records, which also report the bond and insurance the state has on file.

Compliance monitoring

Expiry tracked on every policy and registration, with automatic email and SMS reminders. Anyone outstanding can't be assigned.

Crew scheduling

Each crew keeps its own calendar, so you see which crew is free on the day rather than whether the company is.

Work orders

One per trade, numbered and dated, with its own scope and value. A response deadline you set, re-matching if nobody replies, and numbered change orders when scope moves — the original is never edited.

Labor warranty tracking

Each contractor states their warranty term — 1–10 years or lifetime — and SubSub holds it against every job they finish, so you know later whether a failure is covered.

Callbacks & returns

Raise a callback inside 30 days or a warranty claim after. They confirm the return visit from their own dashboard.

Ratings that compound

Rate each crew on each job. Ratings roll up to the company and feed straight back into who gets matched next time.
